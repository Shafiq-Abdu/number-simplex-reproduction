# Figure 1 Arithmetic — Paper vs Reproduction

| Analysis | What was tested? | Statistical / Analysis Method | Paper Result | Our Reproduction | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Temporal number-coding neurons** | Percentage of neurons significantly decoding numbers 1–9 using temporal spike patterns | 9-way LDA + 200-permutation significance test per neuron; subject-level mean ± SEM | **24.80 ± 1.73%** | **28.61 ± 1.38%** | Higher than paper, but substantial temporal coding reproduced |
| **Firing-rate number-coding neurons** | Percentage of neurons significantly decoding numbers 1–9 using fixed-window firing rate | 9-way LDA + 200-permutation significance test per neuron; subject-level mean ± SEM | **3.36 ± 0.71%** | **4.62 ± 0.84%** | Higher than paper, but much lower than temporal coding in both analyses |
| **Temporal vs FR coding proportion** | Whether subjects have more temporal-coding neurons than FR-coding neurons | **Paired t-test across 11 subjects** | **p < 0.0001** | **t(10) = 17.94, p = 6.20 × 10⁻⁹** | **Significant in both; temporal coding > FR coding** |
| **Regional specificity** | Whether probability of temporal number coding differs across HPC, ENT, AMY, and PARA-HPC | **Binomial GLMM:** `IsTuned ~ Region + (1\|Subject)`; region effect tested with ANOVA | **F(3,550) = 0.43, p = 0.74** | **F(3,550) = 0.052, p = 0.984** | **No significant regional difference in either analysis** |
| **Preferred-number distribution** | Whether coding neurons systematically prefer particular numbers 1–9 across subjects | **Friedman test** | **p = 0.0692** | **χ²(8) = 12.19, p = 0.143** | **No significant preferred-number bias in either analysis** |
| **Preferred-number tie robustness** | Whether the four tied preferred-number assignments could change our Friedman conclusion | **All 24 possible tie assignments + Friedman test** | Not specified | **p range = 0.0755–0.2948; 0/24 significant** | Our nonsignificant preferred-number result is robust to tie handling |
| **Temporal vs FR decoding accuracy (Fig. 1N)** | Whether temporal spike patterns improve single-neuron decoding accuracy relative to firing rate | Neuron-wise temporal vs FR decoding comparison | **+3.79 percentage points** | **+3.842 percentage points** | **Very close quantitative reproduction** |
| **Temporal vs FR decoding significance** | Whether the neuron-wise temporal decoding improvement is statistically significant | **Paired t-test across 548 neurons with paired accuracies** | Significant temporal improvement | **t(547) = 29.65, p = 6.90 × 10⁻¹¹⁶** | **Very strong temporal decoding advantage** |
| **Neurons included in Fig. 1N** | Number of neurons entering the temporal-vs-FR accuracy comparison | Firing-rate threshold / valid decoding estimates | **553 / 554** | **548 / 554 with paired accuracies** | Difference caused by extremely sparse neurons / zero-variance LDA issue in our reconstruction |
