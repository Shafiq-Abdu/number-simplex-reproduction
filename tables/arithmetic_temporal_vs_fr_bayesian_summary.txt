
========================================================================
BAYESIAN ANALYSIS: TEMPORAL vs FIRING-RATE DECODING
========================================================================

ANALYSIS
--------
Arithmetic task
Subject-level comparison of temporal decoding accuracy
versus fixed-window firing-rate (FR) decoding accuracy.

Number of subjects: 11
Total paired neurons: 548

Each subject contributes one mean temporal-minus-FR
decoding accuracy difference.


========================================================================
OBSERVED DATA
========================================================================

Mean temporal decoding accuracy:
14.623%

Mean firing-rate decoding accuracy:
10.673%

Observed mean temporal advantage:
3.950 percentage points

Between-subject SD:
1.306 percentage points

Subjects with temporal > FR:
11/11


========================================================================
BAYESIAN MODEL
========================================================================

Subject-level differences were modeled as:

    Delta_s ~ Normal(mu, sigma^2)

where:

    Delta_s = temporal accuracy - FR accuracy

    mu = population mean temporal decoding advantage

    sigma = between-subject variability


Using the standard noninformative prior for the Normal model
with unknown mean and variance, the marginal posterior for mu is:

    mu | data ~ Student-t(
        df = 10,
        location = 3.950 pp,
        scale = 0.394 pp
    )


========================================================================
POSTERIOR RESULTS
========================================================================

Posterior median:
3.950 percentage points

95% Bayesian credible interval:
[3.073, 4.827] percentage points


Posterior probabilities:

P(mu > 0 pp | data)
    = 99.999923%

P(mu > 1 pp | data)
    = 99.998960%

P(mu > 2 pp | data)
    = 99.971187%

P(mu > 3 pp | data)
    = 98.173444%

P(mu > 3.79 pp | data)
    = 65.323%


========================================================================
INTERPRETATION
========================================================================

The posterior distribution is centered at approximately
3.95 percentage points.

The 95% credible interval is entirely above zero:

    [3.07, 4.83] pp

Under this Bayesian model, there is approximately
99.999923% posterior probability that temporal decoding
has a positive population-level advantage over firing-rate decoding.

There is also approximately
98.17% posterior probability that the mean temporal
advantage exceeds 3 percentage points.

The paper-reported improvement of approximately 3.79 percentage
points lies inside our 95% credible interval.

Therefore, the Bayesian analysis supports the conclusion that
temporal decoding provides a positive and non-trivial improvement
over firing-rate decoding across subjects.


========================================================================
PRIOR-SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
========================================================================

Three zero-centered Normal priors were examined:

    Very broad prior: SD = 10 pp
    Broad prior:      SD = 5 pp
    Skeptical prior:  SD = 2 pp

Despite substantial differences among the prior distributions,
the resulting posterior distributions were very similar and
remained concentrated around approximately 4 percentage points.

Thus, the conclusion that temporal decoding outperforms
firing-rate decoding was not strongly sensitive to these
reasonable prior choices.

NOTE:
The Normal-prior sensitivity calculation treated the observed
between-subject SD as fixed. It is therefore used as a
prior-sensitivity diagnostic rather than as the primary
Bayesian model.


========================================================================
IMPORTANT SCOPE
========================================================================

This Bayesian analysis addresses the relative decoding-performance
claim:

    Temporal decoding > firing-rate decoding.

It does NOT determine what proportion of individual neurons should
be classified as number-coding and does NOT resolve the separate
multiple-comparison/FDR question.
